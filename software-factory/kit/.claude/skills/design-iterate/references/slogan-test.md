# Slogan test (two quick rounds)

Use this for a headline, a slogan or a section title. Don't use a full review round for it. Use the same panel (the roles plus the end customer); each person sees only the first screen's words, for about 5 seconds.

## Round 1

```
You are role-playing one person (your role is in your task). You see a home page's first screen for about 5 seconds. Judge only the slogan and sub-line.

The product: <WHAT IT DOES, ALL STAGES, PRICE IN ONE LINE>.

The owner chose the direction "<DIRECTION>" and wants it refined. Candidates:
- A. ...
- B. ...
(5–6 candidates)

Answer briefly:
1. For each candidate: what it means to you in 5 seconds, and a score out of 10.
2. Does <THE RISKY WORD> read clearly? What do you think it means? Is there a better word?
3. Your best version: a candidate as is, or your own rewrite in the same shape, plus a sub-line of at most 15 words.
4. Anything that would make you stop reading.

Reply with only: your top pick or rewrite, its score, your sub-line, and what <THE RISKY WORD> means to you in three words.
```

## Round 2

Take the winners from round 1 and the panel's own rewrites, and add 2–3 sub-lines. Ask:
- a score for each headline;
- whether any word sounds negative (for example "Fix it" suggesting the product will break);
- their final pair: the headline and the sub-line.

## Reading it

- Look for agreement, not the top single score. Most runs end with 8 or 9 of 10 picking the same pair.
- One person's worry (for example a word that sounds like a recall) goes to the owner as a note, not a veto.
- The owner picks the headline. The test only informs it. One owner kept their own headline and used the winner as the line under it.
