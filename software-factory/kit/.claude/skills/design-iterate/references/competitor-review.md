# Competitor review by the customer panel

Run this before the brief and before any design. It sets the bar from what the customers themselves value, not from references someone happens to like. The product owner can run it too, for positioning and the PRD.

## 1. Identify the stakeholders

- List the **customer segments**: the kinds of company or person who buy.
- List the **roles inside each customer** that can say no: the payer, support, operations, legal or safety, and whoever makes the content.
- Add the **end user** when they aren't the buyer.
- Write one short persona per role: who they are, what they sell or do, how many people, what they fear, and what they measure.

## 2. Pick the competitors

- 4–8 products the buyers would actually compare: direct rivals, the "do nothing" option (for example a PDF or a spreadsheet), and one adjacent leader that buyers admire.
- Use their public pages only: the home page, pricing, the product tour. Take screenshots at phone and laptop sizes.

## 3. The panel scores them

Fresh agents, one per role, skim each competitor page for about a minute, as they would our page:

```
You are <ROLE> (persona in your task). Skim each page below for about a minute: headlines and pictures first.
For each competitor:
1. What do they sell, in one line, from the first screen?
2. Score out of 100: "this convinces me to start".
3. Their strongest point for someone like me: what made me trust or want it?
4. Their weakest point: what put me off or confused me?
5. One thing I'd steal for a page aimed at me.
Then: what none of them gives me that I'd pay for.
Reply with only the scores, the strongest and weakest point per competitor, and the gap.
```

Also run the blind glance test (`design-brief` 5.2) on their first screens, so the visual bar is measured too.

## 4. Write down the bar

- **Strong suits to match or beat**, each with the competitor that showed it and the roles who valued it.
- **Weak spots to avoid**, the same way.
- **The gap:** what no one offers. That becomes the positioning, and often the headline.
- The scores give a baseline: our page should beat the best competitor's average with the same panel.

The brief (`design-brief`) carries this bar in place of "references at the bar". The product owner keeps it with the PRD and re-runs it when a competitor changes or when we pivot.
