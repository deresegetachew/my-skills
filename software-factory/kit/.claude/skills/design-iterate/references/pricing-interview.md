# Buyer interview: pricing

Run this with the panel by segment (section A.3), before the price goes on the page. Brand roles answer it; the end customer doesn't. Show at most three of the simplest models. Ask each person for their own numbers, labelled as their estimate.

---

You are role-playing one buyer (your role is in your task). The product: `<PRODUCT IN 3 LINES, ALL STAGES>`.

The company is choosing between these prices:
- `<MODEL A, e.g. one price per unit a month, everything included>`
- `<MODEL B, e.g. a one-time fee per unit plus a small monthly fee>`
- `<MODEL C, if any>`

Answer as that person, with your own numbers:
1. **Your size:** how many units would you put on it (products, seats…), how many new ones a year, and how much use each one gets a month (typical and bestseller).
2. **Your bill under each model.** Which would you sign, and why? What amount feels fair, and what amount makes you say no?
3. **Limits.** If a unit gets far more use than usual, what should happen? What would make you angry (a hard stop, a surprise bill, a charge for bots)?
4. **What's included.** Should every feature be in one price, or is something an add-on? Never price per what (per claim, per scan, per language)?
5. **When you stop paying.** What must keep working? What may stop?
6. **Your verdict:** the one model and amount you'd sign today, and your score out of 100 for it.

Write your answer to the file named in your task. Reply with only: your size, the model and amount you'd sign, your no-go amount, what must happen when you go over, and your score.

---

**Then, before the page:**
- Work out the cost per unit at each buyer's use (hosting per open, model calls per draft), under the cheapest sensible host.
- Show the owner each buyer's bill under each model in one table, and the margin.
- Research the left-digit effect (for example 9.99 against 11.99) and the approval thresholds in the buyers' companies, if the price is near one.
- Expect most buyers to prefer one number they can predict. Keep limits in the terms, not on the price card, when the cost per unit allows it.
