# Buyer interview: what you need to hear (risk)

Run this before the first build round, with the same five roles as the review panel. The agents get no screenshots: replace `<PRODUCT IN 3 LINES>` with what the product does, who approves what, and the price. List under "Already decided" the facts the owner has confirmed, and under "Open questions" the topics the owner hasn't settled.

---

You are role-playing one buyer (your role is in your task). Here is the product: `<PRODUCT IN 3 LINES>`.

The company is deciding what to commit to. Before they write the page, they want to know what you need to hear. Be concrete and honest, as that buyer would be. Don't be polite, and don't ask for the impossible.

Already decided. React to each briefly: is it enough, and what wording would you trust?
- `<A. …>`
- `<B. …>`

Open questions. For each one give the answer you need to hear before you start, what would make you start today, your dealbreaker, and how many points (out of 100) it is worth to you.
1. What should the company tell you about itself? Who, where, contact, size, support hours: what actually matters to you?
2. Your data: what must the company say about how your content and your customers' data are used?
3. Proof: there are no customers yet. What would you accept instead of a result?
4. `<a product-specific question, e.g. what the support email must carry>`
5. `<a product-specific question, e.g. translations of safety text>`
6. Integrations and dates: which one or two matter to you? Is "available on request" or a quarter-level date enough?
7. Anything else that would stop you starting, which nobody has asked about.

Then give:
- one line: the single promise that would most make you start today;
- one line: your score now, out of 100, if the company answered each question the way you want.

Write your answer to the file named in your task. Reply with only: your one-line promise, your score if answered, and the "needs to hear" line for each of questions 1–6.

---

**After the answers come back:** merge what the roles agree on into one list of facts for the owner. For each item give a plain question, a one-line example of what it means, and the points it is worth. When the owner is surprised by an ask, run a one-line "why" round: ask each role why they want it and what single word would satisfy them.
